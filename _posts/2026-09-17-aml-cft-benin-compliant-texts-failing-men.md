# AML/CFT in Benin: compliant texts, failing men

*Standfirst.* Since Law No. 2024-01 of 20 February 2024, Benin has had a framework for combating money laundering, terrorist financing and proliferation (AML/CFT/CPF) that is technically aligned with FATF standards. Recommendations R.16, R.24 and R.25 were reassessed favorably by GIABA in its 5th enhanced follow-up report (2026). Yet the same report confirms the continuation of the enhanced follow-up regime inherited from a mutual evaluation report (2021), which highlighted the weak effectiveness of the 11 immediate outcomes. How can a country whose texts are deemed compliant fail to apply its own system? The documented case presented here — an estate matter whose official documents contradict one another, brought before **nine institutions** between 2020 and 2026, to which an investigative unit must be added — is not the subject of this article: it is its proof. It reveals seven mechanisms through which men, and not texts, neutralize a control system.[1][2][3]

**Thesis.** The chain **detection → reporting → analysis → dissemination → investigation → prosecution → deterrence** is fully written into the law. It breaks down at every link, not because a rule is missing, but because of a failure of implementation: when a supervised operator becomes the source of information for the authority supposed to supervise him, when an alert can be disqualified without being examined, when an announced deliberation is never notified, when a dismissal closes a file without a hearing, when the supervision of a reporting profession cannot even retrieve the file. Compliance is not proclaimed; it is administered. That is where financial sovereignty is decided.

## 1. The case in three facts

- An authentic instrument — the certificate of acquisition of rights dated 20 October 2016 (case 732, folio 21) — records a death on **10 June 2016**, whereas the civil-status death declaration places it on **6 December 2010**: a gap of **5 years, 6 months and 4 days** between two official documents.
- The same declaration records a value of **138,300,000 FCFA** for the properties, whereas a letter from bank [A.] dated 5 October 2011 certifies **60,000,000 FCFA** in annual rents.
- Plot “B” of lot 488 (Gbégamey), which judgment No. 114/14/3e CH-EP of 31 October 2014 states was bequeathed to the heir, is absent from the public registers: the municipal register records it in the name of a third party, and the National Geographic Institute states that it has no document relating to it, according to a compulsion report drawn up by Martin Jean D.C. GOUNADON, bailiff before the Court of Appeal and the First Class Court of First Instance of Cotonou, on 19 December 2017.

These contradictions are materially established by official and authentic instruments, without any documented institutional explanation having, to date, made it possible to resolve them.

## 2. The seven institutional mechanisms

The case is not the object of the analysis; it is what reveals it. Each line of the previous table points to one mechanism, described below together with the applicable text and the documents in the file.

### 2.1 Capture: the supervised operator speaks on behalf of the supervisor

Law No. 2024-01 extends the scope of reporting entities (art. 3) to non-financial professions, including notaries [Law No. 2024-01](https://sgg.gouv.bj/doc/loi-2024-01/). It changes nothing, however, in the proximity relationships within the succession chain, where the same operator drafts the instrument, derives its value and documents its regularity.

Capture occurs when that operator also becomes the information channel and the source of truth about the authority: it is in the Criminal Brigade report that the notary, Me Félix A. BALLEY, states that the data protection authority “would have dismissed” the complainant — without date, without document, without notification. The authority itself never notified any decision: its deliberation of 19 December 2024 is attested by its own response of 13 December 2024, implicitly signed by its president, Luciano Hounkponou, but no decision was sent to the complainant. The supervised party speaks for the supervisor; the supervisor remains silent.

The inversion is complete in that same response of 13 December 2024: to a complainant who announced that he would make the facts public, the authority replies that publication is “at his discretion,” together with the phrase “you will answer for your acts.” The institution charged with protecting discourages the person who reports: the system then operates as a reversed filter.

This inversion of the control relationship echoes, in another field, the question of **[cognitive sovereignty](https://gsfconsultinginternational.com/analyses/ingenierie-confiance-numerique-ohada/)**: an institution ceases to be sovereign when it no longer controls the circuit of decision, notification and contradiction.

### 2.2 The reporting-party lock

In a response dated 27 May 2025 signed by its president, Abdou Rafiou Bello (ref. 2025-450-c/MEF/CENTIF/SG/DAJCI/SA), CENTIF invoked Articles 60 and 96 of Law No. 2024-01: only the persons referred to in Article 3 are qualified to file a suspicious transaction report; the non-reporting third party is redirected to the competent authorities.

That reading is defensible as regards formal admissibility. It does not answer the central question: **is an authority that becomes aware of the facts exempted from transmitting them?**

No. Article 39 of the Code of Criminal Procedure requires “any constituted authority, any public officer or civil servant” who becomes aware of a crime or offence “to inform the Public Prosecutor without delay” [CCP, Law No. 2012-15, art. 39](https://assemblee-nationale.bj/wp-content/uploads/2020/03/l-2012-15-code-de-procedure-penale.pdf). Article 29 of Law No. 2006-14, applicable to facts prior to 2024, likewise required CENTIF to transmit to the prosecutor a report on facts liable to constitute money laundering [Law No. 2006-14, art. 29](https://sgg.gouv.bj/doc/loi-2006-14/download).

The reporting-party lock is convenient for every link in the chain: it disqualifies the alert without examining it and shifts responsibility to authorities that no one identifies. The file contains no trace of any referral to the prosecutor by CENTIF.

### 2.3 Deliberation without decision

Complaint filed on 3 October 2023 with the APDP, acknowledgment of receipt on 14 December 2023, investigated through summonses in 2024, announced as “examined” with deliberation scheduled for 19 December 2024. Yet the Digital Code (Law No. 2017-20) lays down explicit obligations [Digital Code](https://www.afapdp.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/06/Benin-Loi-2017-20-Portant-code-du-numerique-en-Republique-du-Benin.pdf):

- response “as soon as possible and in any event within thirty (30) days” (art. 420);
- information to the complainant “of the action taken” (art. 483, 6°);
- information “within a reasonable time” of the result of investigations (art. 483, 7°);
- publication of decisions (art. 484);
- notice “without delay” to the prosecutor of violations constituting criminal offences (art. 483, 9°).

None of those deadlines produced any effect. Yet the authority’s 2025 report — 20 complaints handled, 64 inspection missions — establishes its processing capacity [APDP, 2025 report](https://service.apdp.bj/actualites/actualites/454) ; [La Nation](https://lanation.bj/numerique/bilan-2025-de-lapdp-benin-782-dossiers-examines-des-controles-renforces). Silence is therefore not an organizational void; it becomes an implicit decision not to process. The announced deliberation became a ghost deliberation, and the only version circulating is that of the supervised operator.

### 2.4 Dismissal without investigation

FATF Recommendation R.35 requires sanctions that are “effective, proportionate and dissuasive” [FATF Recommendations](https://www.fatf-gafi.org/en/publications/Fatfrecommendations/Fatf-recommendations.html) ; [analysis of R.35](https://financialcrime.lu/col_slider/2025-11-26-FATF-Recommendation-35/).

The criminal sequence in the file shows the opposite:

- complaint for forgery and use of forged public documents dated 17 August 2023;
- preliminary investigation during which the named officer of the court — Me Victorin FADE — is never heard, because he did not respond to his summons;
- dismissal on 3 July 2025 (special prosecutor’s report No. 0416/2025), under the authority of Special Prosecutor Elon'm Mario Metonou, on grounds of “inopportunité des poursuites”;
- request for reopening on 24 September 2025 based on a new fact — the notarial letter of 19 September 2007 establishing the prior position of mandatary of that same lawyer;
- reminder on 25 November 2025;
- referral to the Keeper of the Seals, Yvon Détchénou, on 5 January 2026;
- ministerial response of 14 January 2026 (No. 0049/MJL/SP-C): “I invite you, given the dismissal without further action, to proceed by direct summons or by joining as a civil party”;
- supplementary submission by the complainant on 6 February 2026.

The dismissal for expediency is here the discretionary characterization that closes the file without adversarial debate, without any finding on the merits and without conditional hearing. The referral to direct summons transfers the cost of prosecution to the complainant — precisely the person whom the AML/CFT system is supposed to protect. The culture of administrative accommodation replaces the culture of sanction.

### 2.5 The untraceable supervision of non-financial professions

Benin has extended the scope of reporting entities; yet no mechanism made it possible effectively to supervise Me BALLEY, or even to retrieve his file: transfer file untraceable, signatory inspector untraceable, chamber of notaries without documented response.

The contradiction in the values can also be read in the detail of the flows:

| Flow | Gross amount | Deduction | Net | Date | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cheque (1st tranche, 2011 rent) | Not specified in the letter | **TFU: 5,538,249 FCFA** | **24,461,751 FCFA** | 31/01/2011 | Letter from bank [A.] dated 05/10/2011 |
| Transfer (2nd tranche, 2011 rent) | Not specified in the letter | **Tax: 939,139 FCFA** | **29,060,861 FCFA** | 04/08/2011 | Letter from bank [A.] dated 05/10/2011 |
| Documented annual rents | **≈ 60,000,000 FCFA** | — | — | Year 2011 | Letter from bank [A.] dated 05/10/2011 |

If one adopts the assumption — expressly qualified as such — that the **60,000,000 FCFA** in annual rents concerns the same asset or same set of assets as the declared value of **138,300,000 FCFA**, then:

- the **implicit multiple** comes out at **≈ 2.3 years of rent**;
- the **apparent gross yield** comes out at **≈ 43.4% per year**.

These results do not by themselves establish a legal irregularity; they do establish an **apparent economic anomaly** that called, at the very least, for ex officio verification. None was documented — no more so than for the discrepancy in the date of death.

### 2.6 The alert without a recipient

A letter sent to the Executive Secretariat of GIABA in May 2025, supplemented by a *policy brief* in September 2025, remained without acknowledgment of receipt or documented response — a fact observed by the sender.

The absence of acknowledgment of receipt or documented response raises a question as to the way in which civil-society alerts can be taken into account in the process of evaluating effectiveness. The GIABA/FATF evaluation framework distinguishes technical compliance from effectiveness; it has provided no operative status for an alert raised by a litigant — no procedure, no deadline, no follow-up.

The alert thus becomes an institutional non-event. Yet the documented case shows the concrete failure of the very purpose of R.24 on beneficial ownership: an heir’s identity used without a produced mandate, an untraceable file, a plot that does not exist in the public registers.[3]

### 2.7 The investigation reactivated by the judge, not by the administration

The Criminal Brigade was seized on 11 September 2023 (referral No. 2239-2023/MIL/CRIET/PS/SA) of the complaint for forgery and use of forged documents. It summoned the two ministerial officers — notary Me Félix A. BALLEY and lawyer Me Victorin FADE — “in accordance with UEMOA regulations.” The file then stops.

The then brigade chief removed the investigator from the case, “put the file on hold” and took it over himself, before dying on mission on 27 October 2024; the judicial police officer in charge left for training in December 2023; the complainant’s representative remained “without news until 03 April 2024,” the date of a bailiff’s formal notice. The investigation was effectively at a standstill.

It did not restart on its own initiative. It was the notification by the Constitutional Court on 6 March 2025 that triggered it: “the search through the documents stored in the office of the late Head of the Criminal Brigade made it possible to retrieve the referral,” wrote Chief Commissioner KONTO Sabi Fulbert in his observations of 29 April 2025. Within a few weeks — mission order dated 12 March 2025, hearing of the notary on 19 March 2025, reminders — the investigation was resumed.

Two lessons emerge:

- the relaunch was of **judicial**, not administrative, origin;
- the only documented substantive hearing is that of the notary.

Report No. 2 of 19 March 2025 records in particular the following statement by Me BALLEY: “the Beninese tax system is declarative. Moreover, the amounts used were based on estimates made by their late father.” Concerning the use of the heir’s name, the same report attributes to him this reply: “At the level of the personal data protection authority, he was dismissed.”

Lawyer Me FADE, by contrast, was not heard: in a letter dated 17 March 2025, the President of the Bar invoked Article 6 paragraph 2 of Regulation No. 05/CM/UEMOA of 25 September 2014 before the Prosecutor General in order to require prior consultation before any hearing of a lawyer.

The observations file is thus built on the sole word of a supervised operator, to the exclusion of that of his contradictor. And the authentic instruments in the file (bailiff’s reports), which contradict the notary’s word, are disregarded, in breach of the hierarchy-of-evidence rule. The Brigade then takes up the notary’s imputing narrative and writes that “the main objective of the complainant is to thwart the execution of the provisions of the will left by his late father” — which is not unlawful at all and is, on the contrary, expressly provided for by the texts — then concludes that “this judicial decision, having the authority of res judicata, is not subject to appeal,” whereas the judgment of 31 October 2014 and its service expressly bear the statement “appeal period: 01 month,” and an appeal is pending before the Court of Appeal of Cotonou. By ruling in that way, the Brigade exceeds its role as judicial police.

The contradiction becomes obvious with respect to plot “B” of lot 488. On the one hand, the observations state that the heir, on reading the will, “is supposed to hold the documents relating to the said plot.” On the other hand, the Brigade sent to the City of Cotonou requisition No. 60/2025, followed up on 27 March 2025, in order to obtain, by way of compulsion, consultation of the land register relating to that same plot. This step establishes, at the very least, that the heir’s possession of the documents allowing verification of the reality of the deed of disposition was neither demonstrated nor directly accessible. It therefore contradicts the assertion that the heir had effectively disposed of the asset and held the relevant supporting documents. The issue is all the more serious because judgment No. 114/14/3e CH-EP of 31 October 2014 states: “That X has even already disposed of this property,” before inferring from this that he had voluntarily executed the testamentary provisions. The verb “finds” used by the judicial dispositif does not amount to a mere hypothesis: it presents that disposition as a legally established fact. Yet if no deed of disposition is produced, found in the registers or identified by the investigation, that absence deprives the judicial finding of its apparent material support and constitutes a major evidentiary element in support of the complaint for forgery in public documents. It requires, at the very least, identification of the precise act on which the judgment relied, its author, date, object and mode of preservation. Yet it is established by the competent authorities — the National Geographic Institute and the Legal and Land Affairs Directorate of the City of Cotonou — that the allegedly bequeathed asset does not exist.

## 3. The significance for AML/CFT in Benin

### 3.1 An evaluation paradox

CENTIF recorded **529 suspicious transaction reports** in 2025 and **7 confirmed suspicions**.[4] GIABA validates the technical compliance of several recommendations while maintaining the enhanced follow-up inherited from the 2021 mutual evaluation.[1][2][3]

The gap between the two measures — **compliant text** on the one hand, **alert without follow-up** on the other — is precisely what the evaluation struggles to capture: institutions process other people’s files, and neutralize those that concern them.

### 3.3 The macroeconomic cost

Placement on a FATF grey list is associated, according to the IMF, with an average decline of **7.6 percentage points of GDP** in capital inflows, including **–3.0 percentage points of FDI**, **–2.9 percentage points of portfolio investment** and **–3.6 percentage points of other investment**.[7]

That cost is the cost of an external increase in the country’s risk profile. There is also an internal cost that evaluations do not capture: **reverse deterrence**. When a reporting entity observes that a documented alert leads to neither control, nor sanction, nor acknowledgment of receipt, compliance becomes a formality for the entire marketplace — banks, notaries, lawyers, real estate agents. Reputational risk then shifts from the deviant actor to the system as a whole.

To extend this reading into the technological and prudential sphere, see also our analysis of **[agentic AI and financial fraud in Africa](https://gsfconsultinginternational.com/analyses/ia-agentique-fraude-financiere-afrique/)**.

### 3.4 What neighboring countries are doing

| State | Situation | Trajectory |

| Côte d’Ivoire | MER adopted in June 2023 | Active follow-up cycle: 2nd report in 2025, 3rd report in 2026.[9] |
| ESAAMLG region | Multiple enhanced monitoring cases | The common denominator is not the text, but the institutional capacity to respond, publish and sanction.[10] |

The common denominator of successful exits is **operational, not legal**: where institutions respond, sanction and publish, regimes progress; where they remain silent, texts remain a dead letter.

### 3.5 Five operational requirements

1. **Opposable traceability of alerts**  
   Unique registration number, systematic acknowledgment of receipt, published deadlines. Articles 483, 6° and 7° of the Digital Code exist; the point is to turn them into obligations with real effect, including toward the non-reporting declarant.

2. **Publication of decisions and follow-up**  
   APDP deliberations (art. 484), CENTIF-to-prosecutor transmission indicators, grounds for dismissal.

3. **Functional separation**  
   Every decision must be notified directly to the person concerned, with remedies; the supervised operator must never be the information channel about the authority.

4. **Culture of sanction (R.35)**  
   End the dismissal for expediency without a hearing; publish indicators on prosecutions and sanctions.

5. **Status of the civil alert within the GIABA process**  
   Procedure, deadline, traceability, so that documented alerts enter the evaluation of effectiveness on the same footing as technical data.

This issue also connects with GSF analyses on **[the regulation of AI in Africa](https://gsfconsultinginternational.com/analyses/regulation-ia-afrique-souverainete-numerique/)** and on the need for institutional systems that are auditable, reversible and genuinely governed.

## Conclusion

Benin has chosen texts: Law No. 2024-01, the Digital Code, the revised Criminal Code, regional evaluations. That choice is technically defensible — which is what makes the finding all the more severe.

A system whose technical compliance is validated and whose effectiveness remains nil is not under construction: it is **neutralized in its application**. At every link in the chain, the interruption occurs at the same place — where a civil servant, a judge, a notary or an institutional official should have answered.

Financial sovereignty is not proclaimed: it is demonstrated, file by file. The texts are ready. The men remain.
